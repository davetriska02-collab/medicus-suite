## 1 · Executive summary

**Position.** "Starting to match full EHRs" is half-true, and the half that
is true is the valuable half. Against the record-keeping core of an EHR the
suite is not a competitor and never will be — it reads Medicus's own data and
cannot exist without it (§5). But on the **clinician-workflow layer** — the
tooling a GP touches every hour — the suite is not merely matching the field;
on four verified dimensions it is doing things **no product surveyed does at
all**: monitoring currency at the moment of repeat signing, live same-minute
practice-pressure dashboards, severity-triaged results-queue overlay, and a
first-class personal safety-net ledger. The incumbents' pattern is a generic
chassis with the clinical intelligence bought in (Ardens, OptimiseRx, Docman
AI, Rapid Health); the add-on layer is bounded by the host EHRs' extension
points and explicitly cannot reach the signing screen; and the challenger
field is one real product (the suite's own host) plus press releases.

**The three most dangerous competitors.**

1. **Medicus Health / Doctolib** — the host itself. It owns the platform the
   suite rides on, claims weekly ship cadence, and now has >£100m of Doctolib
   money and explicit AI ambitions. Anything the suite proves valuable,
   Medicus can build natively with better data access. This is also the
   suite's growth engine: the suite's addressable market IS Medicus's
   practice count (10 live, 97 implementing across 18 ICBs as of March
   2026), so Doctolib's investment grows the suite's world. Frenemy, not
   just threat.
2. **SystmOne (TPP)** — the only surveyed product with a shipped,
   configurable **results auto-review rules engine** (Oct 2024; one
   documented config auto-processes ~30% of results). Deeper than the
   suite's gated all-normal filer, and native.
3. **Ardens** — not an EHR but the incumbent of the suite's own category:
   claimed 87% penetration, 25–43p/patient/year, exhaustive maintained
   content, real point-of-care alerts. Its ceiling is structural (it cannot
   redraw the host's screens; its EMIS analyser documentedly cannot handle
   initiation/titration monitoring; its dashboards are daily-batch) — but on
   content breadth it is uncatchable and should not be chased.

**Single biggest gap:** the **documents/letters modality**. Docman owns coded
correspondence flow at 1,000+ EMIS practices, SystmOne ships native OCR
document management, Docman's AI Workflow Assistant is live at early-adopter
scale, and Medicus itself claims auto-extraction from post. The suite has
nothing here, and it was also the panel's top clinical ask (D2). Everything
needed to start is one scoping session away.

**Single biggest opportunity:** **own the signing moment and say so.** Across
two incumbent EHRs, the host, three challengers and six add-ons, nothing
verified puts monitoring currency, renal context or request-vs-flag joins
into the act of authorising a repeat. The suite shipped exactly that
(v3.155–3.159). The window exists because add-ons _cannot_ redraw the signing
screen and the incumbents' release cadence is measured in years (EMIS-X:
seven years to a companion app). The one player who could close it is
Medicus/Doctolib — expect a 12–24 month window, and spend it extending the
lead (D8 slices) and evidencing it.

## 3 · Gap analysis

### 3a · Table stakes we are missing

1. **Documents/letters workflow** — every serious comparator has it native,
   bought-in, or credibly claimed; the suite has nothing. Also the panel's
   top clinical ask (D2). Blocked only on a one-session live scoping probe.
2. **Early-request / over-use flags at signing** — EMIS ships this natively
   and configurably; it was also Tom's D8 ask. We show monitoring risk but
   not request anomalies. Embarrassing precisely because our signing surface
   is otherwise the best in the field.
3. **QOF whole-register position and £ projection** — native indicator
   tracking in both incumbents, £-projection solved by Ardens Manager at
   18p/patient/year. Our per-patient QOF awareness doesn't answer Janet's
   "will we hit the threshold by March". Gated on register-enumeration
   scoping (D4).
4. **External clinical-safety assurance** — the artefacts exist (hazard log,
   intended-purpose statement) but no DCB0129 assessment or DTAC has been
   externally made. The moment the suite is used beyond the authoring
   practice, this is the first procurement question. Not code — process.
5. **Batch recall execution** — finding the cohort is shipped (Sweep); the
   invite→book loop is manual. Ardens+AccuRx practices have a (three-product,
   manually-glued) loop. Partially out of intended purpose (comms are
   Medicus's) — the honest slice is exporting actionable cohort lists into
   Medicus's own recall/messaging tools.

### 3b · Parity (one line each)

- Per-patient QOF indicator awareness: level with native EHR views; theirs
  cover the whole register, ours is live at point of care.
- Results filing throughput: SystmOne ahead on automation breadth, suite
  ahead on triage UX and honesty states; net a draw on different axes.
- Request triage: suite's queue chips ≈ partner products (Rapid Health,
  PATCHS) in function; theirs sit at intake, ours on the worked queue.
- Task creation into the record system: parity with all (we drive Medicus's
  own workflow; routing/escalation is properly the EHR's job).
- Prescribing-safety combinations: our 23 display-only alerts trail FDB
  Multilex CDS in breadth; PINCER-derived content is comparable to
  OptimiseRx's sources. Partial, honestly.

### 3c · White space (verified unserved by the whole field)

1. **The signing moment** — monitoring currency, renal context,
   requested-drug joins at the point of authorising a repeat. Nobody. Already
   ours.
2. **Live same-minute practice-ops dashboards** — Ardens Manager is a daily
   import, Apex is retrospective, EMIS-X Analytics is ICB-tier, TPP's RTA is
   an event feed requiring local infrastructure. Condor is alone here.
3. **Severity-triaged results inbox** — no comparator orders the filing queue
   by clinical risk. Ardens explicitly declined the space on safety grounds
   (which validates our fail-closed framing rather than the space being bad).
4. **Initiation/titration monitoring** — Ardens' EMIS analyser documents its
   own inability; our rules engine already handles worsening-result interval
   shortening. Under-evidenced in our own docs; say it louder.
5. **The trainee layer** (Priya's D6) — nothing in the field attempts
   trainee-shaped output. Genuine novelty if built identifier-free.
6. **First-class personal follow-up ledger** — EMIS's national toolkit is a
   template+diary+admin-reports contraption; ours is one keypress and a
   lapse-surfacing Today line. Shipped (pending merge).

**Stress-test summary.** Every leapfrog survives except where
Medicus/Doctolib is the counterparty — and that risk is structural to being
an overlay on a well-funded host. The strategic answer is recorded here
plainly: the suite's defensibility against the host is **velocity, clinical
honesty (the hazard-log/honest-state doctrine a platform vendor moving fast
will not replicate soon), and being the host's best demo**. A Medicus
practice running the suite is a better Medicus sale; the rational Doctolib
move is partnership or acquisition, not a copy-and-kill. Worth saying out
loud before the 12–24 month window starts closing.

## 5 · What an EHR is that we are not (the honest section)

The suite scores zero, permanently and by design, on: the legal patient
record; the prescribing/signing _act_ (EPS, controlled drugs, PIN);
appointments as system-of-record; patient communications (SMS/NHS App);
registration/PDS; consultations/coding as authorship; multi-organisation
data sharing; national integrations (e-RS, GP2GP, EPS); TIF/framework
assurance; and existing-without-Medicus. "Matching an EHR" therefore means:
**on the workflow layer the clinician feels all day, the suite now matches or
beats what £150k+ of EHR-plus-add-ons delivers; as an EHR, it is not one, and
its entire market is currently the host's ~10 live / 97 implementing
practices.** That last fact cuts both ways: tiny today, and growing at
Doctolib's spend rate tomorrow.

## 6 · Verdict on Dave's framing

"Starting to match in terms of features" — verified TRUE for the clinician
workflow layer, with four dimensions where the suite leads the entire
surveyed field outright, and three table-stakes holes (documents, QOF
position, request-anomaly flags) that are all already on the dream-features
plan. FALSE, cheerfully and permanently, for the EHR core — and the correct
strategy is not to close that gap but to stay the best workflow layer on the
fastest-growing host, with the DCB0129/DTAC paperwork ready for the day
another Medicus practice asks to install it.
