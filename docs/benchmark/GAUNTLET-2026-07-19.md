## 1 · Executive summary

**Position, one paragraph.** The suite's July verdict holds: on the
clinician-workflow layer it leads the surveyed field outright on the signing
moment, live ops, severity-triaged results and the personal follow-up
ledger, and it is not (and will never be) an EHR. What changed in twelve
days is the **direction of pressure**. The host stayed quiet (no shipped
Medicus feature delta found), but the field moved: Ardens is now arriving on
Medicus itself (Manager live via IM1, Clinical templates in testing), EMIS
shipped a native ambient scribe, AccuRx crossed from messaging into AI inbox
summarisation, OneAdvanced consolidated Docman + PATCHS under an AI platform
with its own NHS-trained LLM, and NHS England put autonomous triage into the
NHS App at national scale. The squeeze is real but none of it lands on
Medicus practices *today*, which means the suite still has the only
workflow overlay on the fastest-growing host, and roughly a
12–24 month window that is now visibly being spent by other people.

**Three most dangerous competitors (updated).**

1. **Ardens** — promoted from category incumbent to *incoming neighbour*.
   Ardens Manager already ingests Medicus data via IM1 bulk pairing
   (product spec v1.2, 2026-04-23; verified 2026-07-19) and Ardens Clinical
   for Medicus is in template testing with a phased rollout "over the coming
   months". When it lands, the suite is no longer the only clinical content
   on Medicus. Its structural ceilings stand (no signing-screen access,
   daily-batch dashboards, documented titration gap).
2. **Medicus Health / Doctolib** — unchanged threat logic, quiet window. The
   Doctolib Consultation Assistant remains claimed-only for the UK, with a
   stated goal of automating up to 50% of routine secretarial tasks by
   end-2026. The window is intact but the clock runs.
3. **OneAdvanced (Docman + PATCHS)** — one vendor now spans documents,
   online/phone triage, and an AI platform ("IQ", first wave 2026-06-02;
   sovereign NHS-primary-care LLM announced 2026-06-09). GP Workflow
   Assistant still early-adopter (claimed 143 practices), not GA. None of it
   touches Medicus practices yet.

**Single biggest gap:** unchanged and now more exposed — the
**documents/letters modality**. EMIS is running EMIS-X document-management
webinars for GP practices (16 and 22 July 2026), Docman's AI assistant
grows, Anima ships document processing, SystmOne has native OCR. The suite
still has nothing, twelve days after the July report called it the top
table-stake and the panel's top clinical ask. The scoping probe (M1, effort
S) has not been run and gates everything.

**Single biggest opportunity:** unchanged — **own the signing moment and
say so**, and now also **out-trace Ardens before it arrives**: the suite's
defence against imported content on its own host is per-chip traceability
("why did this fire"), which Ardens' closed content model cannot match.

## 3 · Gap analysis (updated)

### 3a · Table stakes still missing

1. **Documents/letters workflow** — unchanged, gap widening as EMIS-X and
   Docman AI advance. M1 probe still not run. (GP + admin; top ask.)
2. **Early-request / over-use flags at signing** — unchanged; EMIS native.
   Our signing surface is still the best in the field and still lacks the
   most obviously adjacent chip.
3. **QOF whole-register position / £** — unchanged, and Ardens Manager on
   Medicus now delivers a version of this to our own host's practices at
   18p/patient/year. (Practice-manager lens, so out of this run's target,
   but noted: the gap is now being filled *on Medicus* by someone else.)
4. **External clinical-safety assurance** — DTAC tracker exists
   (`docs/DTAC-STATUS.md`) but is a draft pinned at v3.84.2, CSO signature
   pending. Unchanged. First procurement question the day a second practice
   asks.
5. **Batch recall execution (cohort-export slice)** — unchanged; and with
   Ardens Manager live on Medicus, the comparison on recall tooling is no
   longer hypothetical.

### 3b · Parity (one line each)

- Request triage: our ~80 queue rules vs AccuRx's new AI summaries — theirs
  summarise, ours red-flag; different jobs, converging surface. Watch.
- Results filing: unchanged draw (SystmOne automation breadth vs our triage
  UX and honesty states).
- Task creation, per-patient QOF awareness, prescribing-combination alerts:
  unchanged from July, one line each stands.

### 3c · White space (still unserved by the whole field)

Unchanged from July, all six verified still open: the signing moment,
same-minute ops dashboards, severity-triaged results inbox,
initiation/titration monitoring, the trainee layer, the personal follow-up
ledger (now shipped and alone in the field). New this run: **record
duplicate cleanup** appears to be a seventh — no surveyed product offers
GP2GP-duplication compare/merge/remove tooling.

## 4 · The five suggested builds (the exceed plan, this run's deliverable)

Scored on the balanced scorecard: **T** time saved, **S** clinical safety,
**£** money — each ●●● (major) to ○ (negligible). Target users per item.
Effort: S <2h · M half-day · L 1–2 days · XL needs breakdown.

### 1. Letter Lens v1 — run the M1 probe this week, then Discharge Delta

**What:** one-session scoping probe of the Medicus documents view (M1,
effort S), then the smallest honest slice of M2: for a discharge summary or
clinic letter open on screen, a display-only diff of medication lines
against the current repeat list — started / stopped / changed / **UNMATCHED,
fail-loud**. No coding, no filing, no OCR; reads what the clinician is
already looking at.
**Users:** GPs (letter review) + admin (document workflow). **T ●●● S ●●● £ ●**
**Effort:** M1 = S; Delta v1 = XL (break down after probe). CSO + hazard log
mandatory before build.
**Neutralises:** Docman AI Workflow Assistant (claimed 143 practices),
SystmOne native OCR, the EMIS-X document-management push.
**Why now:** it has been the #1 gap in two consecutive Gauntlets, it is the
panel's top clinical ask, and three competitors advanced on it in twelve
days. The probe is two hours and gates everything; not running it is the
single most expensive inaction on this sheet.

### 2. Early-request and quantity-anomaly chips in the Signing Queue (M3/D8)

**What:** at signing, flag requests that arrive materially early against
issue duration, and quantity step-ups — workflow anomaly flags computed from
issue history the suite already reads, not clinical rules (no keeper gate
for this slice).
**Users:** GPs (every signing session). **T ●● S ●●● £ ●**
**Effort:** M–L.
**Neutralises:** EMIS's native compliance warnings — the one signing-adjacent
feature an incumbent ships that we lack.
**Why now:** the signing surface is the suite's flagship lead (nothing
surveyed puts *any* context into the signing act) and this is its most
obvious missing chip. Closing it converts "best in field with a gap" into
"complete", and it compounds the L1 evidence-note story.

### 3. Grey-zone results context — last-3-values inline on non-normal rows (B2)

**What:** on the results queue, for each non-normal analyte, an inline
sparkline-grade context chip: previous three values with dates, plus
explained-by flags the engine already computes (known CKD, on-drug expected
change). Display-only, honest not-assessed states preserved.
**Users:** GPs (results sessions). **T ●●● S ●● £ ○**
**Effort:** L.
**Neutralises:** SystmOne's auto-review lead, from the opposite side — their
engine automates the normal third; this makes the borderline two-thirds
faster and safer, where auto-filing rightly refuses to go.
**Why now:** it deepens an existing white-space lead (severity-triaged
inbox) rather than opening a new front, and it is the highest
minutes-per-day item on the sheet after documents.

### 4. Recall cohort-export slice (M7) — Sweep list into Medicus's own tools

**What:** one-click export of a Sweep cohort (the patients failing a rule,
with reason and due-date) as a paste-ready list/CSV shaped for Medicus's
recall and messaging workflow. No sending, no writing — comms remain
Medicus's job, inside intended purpose.
**Users:** reception/admin (the recall grind) + practice income. **T ●● S ● £ ●●●**
**Effort:** M.
**Neutralises:** the Ardens+AccuRx three-product recall glue — and, newly
urgent, **Ardens Manager now live on Medicus**, which will otherwise become
the default recall-list source at our own host.
**Why now:** it is the cheapest item on the sheet that serves reception
directly, and the twelve-day delta turned its competitor column from
"elsewhere" to "arriving here".

### 5. "Why did that fire?" workings view on every chip (B4/D5)

**What:** click any chip (monitoring, result, triage, QOF) and see the
workings: rule, matched terms, observed values with dates, threshold, and
the rule's provenance/currency stamp. The engine already holds all of it
(`test-rule-match-evidence.js`, evaluation-trace work); this is surfacing,
not new inference.
**Users:** GPs (trust at the moment of decision) + trainees later (D6
substrate). **T ● S ●● £ ○** — plus a strategic multiplier no scorecard
cell captures: it is the anti-Ardens play.
**Effort:** M.
**Neutralises:** Ardens' content-trust moat, precisely as Ardens Clinical
lands on Medicus. Their content is authoritative but closed; a chip that
shows its own evidence chain is something a template pack architecturally
cannot answer.
**Why now:** in July this was a nice-to-have; with Ardens in template
testing on our host, traceability becomes the differentiation story, and it
also de-risks every other alert the suite ships (a wrong chip that shows its
workings is a bug report; a wrong chip that doesn't is lost trust).

### Stress test (mandatory, per item)

1. **Letter Lens:** why hasn't anyone done display-only discharge-vs-repeat
   diff on Medicus? Nobody else runs *on* Medicus at all; Docman/EMIS solve
   it at the filing layer for other EHRs, and their business model is the
   document pipeline, not the in-record moment. Honest risk: Medicus itself
   claims document auto-extraction (marketing-graded, adopter quote only) —
   if that ships natively, v1 is partially obsoleted; the diff-against-repeats
   view likely still survives as the clinical layer on top. Proceed.
2. **Early-request chips:** EMIS did do it — natively, years ago. This is a
   match item, not a leapfrog; no stress test needed beyond "why hasn't
   Ardens": add-ons cannot reach the signing screen (architectural, documented
   in July). Proceed.
3. **Grey-zone context:** TPP could extend auto-review to show history
   inline but its investment is in automating normals, not decorating
   abnormals (a rules engine sells; a context chip doesn't demo). Risk low;
   the 30 July TPP "Clinical AI" webinar is the thing to watch. Proceed,
   re-check after 30 July.
4. **Cohort export:** trivially copyable by Ardens Manager (it already owns
   the dashboard). The defence is locality: our list is same-minute, theirs
   is daily-batch, and ours is free and already installed. Risk accepted;
   the item is cheap enough that even a 12-month shelf life pays.
5. **Workings view:** why has no one? Because incumbent content vendors
   *cannot* — showing workings would expose licensed content logic
   (Ardens/FDB business-model constraint), and EHR-native alerts are built
   on closed CDS. An in-house rules engine with its own provenance stamps is
   structurally the only place this is cheap. Risk: near zero from
   competitors; the real risk is our own rule-quality being exposed — which
   is exactly the point. Proceed.

**Sequencing note (not asked for, but it falls out of the scoring):** the
M1 probe (S effort) should precede everything; items 2, 4 and 5 (M to M–L)
are independent and parallelisable; item 3 (L) next; Letter Lens v1 (XL)
scoped from the probe's findings.
