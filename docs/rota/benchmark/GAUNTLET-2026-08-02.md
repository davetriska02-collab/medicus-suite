# The Gauntlet: delta refresh, 2026-08-02

Run date: 2026-08-02. Subject: Medicus Rota Manager **v1.10.0**, scored exactly as shipped
(roadmap items count as absent). This is a **delta refresh** against the full run of
2026-06-11 (which scored v1.6.0): the comparable set, category structure and beat dimensions
(automation depth, EHR/appointment-book integration, UX, time-to-live) are unchanged; only
cells with verified movement are restated. Read the June report first; this one diffs it.

**Purpose of this run:** verify the feature suite is acceptable ahead of subsuming the Rota
Manager into the Medicus Suite as the overarching extension. Verdict at the end.

Evidence note: as in June, every vendor site 403-blocks datacentre fetches (this round the

## Executive summary

Nothing moved against us. In seven weeks, none of the six GP-native comparables shipped a
verifiable new capability: RotaMaster shows an active release pipeline (two undated Data API
releases) but no clinical-system integration and no answer to its clunky-desktop-UI
complaint; Lantum's genuine movement (In-Genius AI rostering, InCheck, the DRS acquisition)
all pre-dates the June baseline and remains confined to secondary care; Tempo is still
SystmOne-only batch export with no EMIS or Medicus work; Practice Index, Agilio and GPRota
were static. Meanwhile our column strengthened in exactly the places the June report said to
push: four of the five Tier 1 "match" items shipped (WTD checks, TOIL, timesheet CSV,
browser notifications), and two Tier 2 "beat" items shipped deep (the one-click setup wizard
and demand-curve planning, both category-unique). The decisive category, live rota against
appointment book, remains ours alone.

The three most dangerous competitors are unchanged in order: Tempo, RotaMaster, Lantum. The
single biggest gap is unchanged and untouched: the commercial wrapper (pricing, support
channel, DSPT/DTAC posture, true multi-user accounts), which should now be assessed against
**DTAC v2** (old form retired 2026-04-06). The single biggest strategic event of the window
is not a competitor at all: **Doctolib acquired Medicus** (announced 2026-05-06, £100m+ UK
investment plan, further disclosure reported 2026-07-13). No Medicus rostering product
exists or is announced, but the acquirer changes the write-back partnership calculus in both
directions: more resource to open APIs to partners, and more resource to build rostering
natively. The June founder intelligence ("Medicus is not building rostering") pre-dates the
acquisition settling and should be re-confirmed.

One tailwind worth recording: the 2026/27 GP contract (in force since April) obliges
practices to plan capacity and provide workforce data to ICBs. A rota tool with real
demand-curve planning from the live appointment book is now aligned with a contractual duty,
not just a convenience.

Unchanged in content from June (write-back; same-day scramble automation with live patient

- **Write-back risk profile has changed.** The Doctolib acquisition cuts both ways: a

  (fatal to the moat). The June founder intelligence that Medicus is not building rostering

  founder relationship before investing further in write-back groundwork. Until confirmed,
  treat write-back as parked and keep extending the read-side lead (reconciliation,

## Verdict: acceptable to integrate into the Medicus Suite?

**Yes, without reservation on feature grounds.** At v1.10.0 the product leads every
GP-native comparable on automation depth (solver, guardrails, wizard, demand planning), is
alone in the decisive category (live appointment-book awareness), and has closed the
checkbox gaps (WTD, TOIL, timesheets) that would have embarrassed it in a line-by-line
comparison. No competitor movement in seven weeks weakens the case.

The open gaps (commercial wrapper, phone push, payroll depth, multi-user accounts) are
orthogonal to the integration question: none is made worse by subsumption, and two are made
better. Integration gives the rota tool the suite's distribution (one install, one Medicus
login, one backup envelope) and puts the suite's existing alert-strip and polling
infrastructure under the rota's future event-driven reconciliation, which is the top
remaining Tier 2 item. The strategic argument runs the same direction: with Doctolib now
owning Medicus, a single coherent suite with the rota inside it is a stronger partnership
asset than two separate extensions.

Condition attached to the verdict: the integration must not regress the rota app's shipped
UX (the v1.9.0 Atelier pass is part of the scored surface), and the suite's backup
conventions must absorb the `rota.*` keys so the "versioned backup" cell stays honest.
