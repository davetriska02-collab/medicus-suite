# The Gauntlet: Medicus Rota Manager vs the GP-native rota market

Run date: 2026-06-11. Subject: Medicus Rota Manager v1.6.0, scored exactly as shipped (roadmap
items count as absent). Comparable set: GP-native rota tools only, as scoped. Beat dimensions,
as ranked by Dave: automation depth, EHR/appointment-book integration, UX and time-to-live.

Evidence note: every vendor site in the set blocks datacentre fetches (HTTP 403), so competitor
findings come from search-indexed vendor primary sources (help centres, knowledge bases, G-Cloud
listings, app stores) retrieved 2026-06-11. "Verified" therefore means seen in indexed vendor
documentation, one notch below eyes-on-docs. Two findings additionally rest on founder
intelligence dated 2026-06-11: Tempo remains SystmOne-only, and Medicus is not building
rostering. Both are consistent with everything in the public record.

## Executive summary

Our position in one paragraph: Medicus Rota Manager is, today, the only rota product in UK
general practice with any live clinical-system awareness, and the only one that can set itself
up from the appointment book (clinician import, pattern inference, room inference). On
automation depth we already exceed every GP-native competitor: nobody else ships a
deterministic rota solver, leave guardrails with seasonal caps, a ranked same-day cover
assistant, and a CQC evidence pack in one product. What we are not, yet, is a commercial
product: no pricing, no support channel, no DSPT/DTAC posture, no mobile app, no payroll
export, and a shared-folder sync that is honest but not enterprise multi-user.

The three most dangerous competitors:

1. **Tempo (GPnetworks)**: the only other vendor that understands sessions, duty doctors and
   the appointment-book problem. Its SystmOne batch export is the market's sole integration
   prior art. If anyone notices Medicus first, it is Tempo.
2. **RotaMaster**: the incumbent benchmark for breadth. Registrar allocation wizard, hard WTD
   blocking, the deepest sickness/Bradford/RTW stack, payroll export, mature staff app.
   Procurement comparisons get measured against this list.
3. **Lantum**: owns the cover economics end to end (marketplace, banks, next-day pay,
   automated pension forms) and has a genuine AI solver, albeit deployed in secondary care.

Single biggest gap: the commercial wrapper. Pricing, support, compliance posture and a real
multi-user story are table stakes in procurement and we score absent on all of them.

Single biggest opportunity: the moat is open and undefended. One vendor has batch integration
with one EHR; nobody has live sync with anything; nobody has touched Medicus; Medicus is not
building rostering. Write-back, when the partnership lands, ends the category. Until then,
event-driven live reconciliation extends a lead nobody else is even racing for.

## Gap analysis

### Table stakes we are missing

1. **Mobile app and push notifications.** RotaMaster and Lantum both ship apps; swaps and
   gap-fills die without push. Our ICS export is good but passive.
2. **Payroll and timesheet export.** RotaMaster exports straight into payroll systems;
   Practice Index bundles a timesheet manager. We produce nothing money-shaped.
3. **WTD checks.** RotaMaster hard-blocks WTD breaches and sells on it. We have none
   (our house style would warn rather than block, but absent is absent).
4. **TOIL.** Tempo and Agilio both treat TOIL as a first-class leave type.
5. **The commercial wrapper.** Pricing, support, DSPT/DTAC posture, proper multi-user
   accounts. Not code gaps, but they decide procurements.

### Parity

Leave request workflow, multi-site, staff self-service basics, rooms (vs Tempo/Agilio/GPRota),
audit trail (vs Agilio). One sentence each is all these merit: we are level, nobody wins here.

### Where competitors are simply better

- **Lantum on cover economics**: marketplace, bank, Rocketpay, automated pension forms. Years
  of network effects and payment infrastructure we should not chase head-on.
- **RotaMaster on HR/finance depth**: payroll runs, overtime, expenses, mileage, hard WTD.
  An enterprise back-office we do not have.
- **Practice Index on price and distribution**: £425/year published, 15,000+ practice-manager
  members. Procurement-trivial in a way we are not.

### White space nobody covers well

1. Live rota↔appointment-book reconciliation (only us; Tempo's batch S1 export is the sole
   adjacent art and is fragile by its own documentation).
2. EHR-driven setup: clinician import, pattern inference, room inference (only us).
3. Demand-aware planning from real appointment data (Practice Buddy claims it, nothing
   verified anywhere).
4. CQC evidence generation (GPRota's Rota Checker validates staffing; nobody produces the
   inspection artefact).
5. Anything Medicus (nobody, including Medicus).

### Stress test (why have competitors not already done the leapfrogs?)

- **Write-back**: Tempo's own docs show why this is hard against legacy EHRs: their S1 export
  breaks if two-letter appointment codes mismatch, because S1 has no real API surface for
  this. EMIS is no better; Tempo ships written setup guides instead of software for it.
  Medicus is FHIR-native with open-by-default APIs, but it is one year past NHS approval with
  four early-adopter sites: too small for incumbent roadmaps to notice. We already speak its
  API and the founder relationship is live. Genuine risk: Medicus builds rostering natively
  on its weekly-release cadence. Mitigation is the partnership conversation already in
  progress, and the founder intelligence (2026-06-11) is that they are not building it.
- **Scramble automation**: requires reading the live appointment book at the moment of
  sickness. Every incumbent's architecture is rota-only; they cannot see the book at all, so
  the feature is not buildable for them without first doing the integration they have skipped
  for a decade. Not copyable within a quarter.
- **Continuous CQC score**: incumbents sell predominantly to federations and OOH providers,
  where CQC evidence sits with a governance team, not the rota tool. Single-practice CQC
  anxiety is our buyer's problem, not theirs. Risk of fast copy is real but low value to
  them; for us it compounds with the audit trail and rules engine they also lack.
- **PCN EA hub rota**: needs cross-organisation appointment visibility, which is GP Connect
  provider-side work nobody has done for rostering. Honest demotion: this stays XL and
  sequenced behind write-back; if it proves easy it is also easy for Lantum, so it must not
  carry the plan.
