## 1. Purpose of this notice

This Clinical Safety Notice summarises the clinical safety information that every user, and every deploying GP practice, must read and accept before installing or using Medicus Suite. It is binding and constitutes a condition of use, alongside:

- `docs/HAZARD-LOG.md` — the full hazard register and residual risk assessment
- `docs/sentinel-DISCLAIMER.txt` — the full disclaimer and legal terms
- `docs/INTENDED-PURPOSE.md` — the frozen intended-purpose statement

In the event of any conflict between this notice and the disclaimer, the disclaimer prevails.

## 2. Intended purpose

Medicus Suite is a Google Chrome extension that operates alongside the Medicus electronic patient record (EPR). It is overwhelmingly a read-and-display tool: it reads data already present in the clinician's authenticated Medicus session and re-displays a reorganised, summarised view in a side panel and on configurable on-page overlays. It additionally provides a small, enumerated set of **user-initiated write actions** — appointment booking, general-task creation, inbound-document filing, normal-lab-result filing, routine-prescription re-assignment, and problem-list / duplicate-entry tidying — each triggered by a user on the record in front of them, confirmed at the point of commit, and executed through Medicus's own endpoints or its own on-screen controls so that Medicus's validation, access control and audit trail fire exactly as if the user had done the work by hand. Those write paths are stated in full, with their controls, at **section 6.1**. It applies arithmetic threshold checks (for example: "the most recent HbA1c was recorded 114 days ago; the configured monitoring interval is 90 days; the indicator is therefore overdue") and surfaces the result as a colour-coded indicator.

From v1.6 onwards, the extension additionally includes the **Patient Record Visualiser** — a full-page dashboard that processes a locally-held Medicus EPR export PDF to produce summary analytics. These include continuity-of-care indices, investigation trend charts with clinical zone bands, high-risk drug monitoring compliance, a computed Electronic Frailty Index (eFI), PINCER-style prescribing safety prompts, and QOF register review status. All Visualiser processing occurs locally in the user's browser; no patient data is transmitted externally at any stage.

From v3.13.0–v3.14.0 the live Triage Lens side panel additionally surfaces, on the record/medications view, **deterministic prescribing-combination prompts** (STOPP/START-style: NSAID + anticoagulant/antiplatelet, "triple whammy" NSAID + ACEi/ARB + diuretic, benzodiazepine/Z-drug in age ≥80), a **risk-tool signpost chip** that links to the official QRISK3 / QCancer / eFI calculators (it computes no score), and **NHS Pharmacy First pathway signposting** snippets. These are supplementary prompts to _review and verify_, framed in the same way as the Visualiser's PINCER prompts — they are not clinical recommendations, diagnoses, or triage decisions. The practice-facing **Custom Alert Builder** also gained (v3.15.0–v3.16.0) an engine-backed live preview and save-time schema validation, so an author can see whether a rule they are building would fire against a test patient before saving it; from v3.22.0 the builder exposes full engine parity including `requiresProblem`/`requiresAnyProblem`, `excludeIfProblem`, sex, age range, `mustNotBePresent` drug-absence gate, and per-test SNOMED aliases.

## 3. Intended users

Medicus Suite is intended for use by trained personnel working within a GP practice that uses the Medicus clinical system, namely:

- **General Practitioners (GPs)** — for clinical memory-aid use during patient consultations
- **Practice Nurses and Advanced Practitioners** — for clinical memory-aid use within their scope of practice
- **Practice Managers and Administrators** — for capacity, slot, submissions, activity, and referrals views (non-clinical workflows)
- **QOF leads / clinical coding leads** — for QOF status review (verified against Medicus)
- **Referral coordinators** — for the Referrals Tracker (verified against Medicus)

All users must:

1. Be authorised by the practice to access the patient data they are viewing
2. Hold an active and valid login to the Medicus EPR
3. Have read and accepted this notice, the hazard log, and the full disclaimer
4. Operate within their own professional scope and the policies of their practice

The extension must **not** be used by patients, by personnel not authorised to access the underlying records, or on any system other than Google Chrome stable channel on a workstation logged in as the named user.

## 4. Regulatory status

**Medicus Suite is not a medical device.**

The author asserts, on a good-faith reading of MHRA guidance on Software as a Medical Device current at the date of this notice, that Medicus Suite falls outside the scope of the UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002 (as amended). This assertion rests on the following facts:

- The software does not produce any output that constitutes a diagnosis, treatment recommendation, prognosis, triage decision, or clinical interpretation of patient data.
- The software re-displays values already visible to the clinician in the source record. It does not transform those values into new clinical information.
- The software does not perform any calculation whose output is intended to drive a clinical decision. Threshold comparisons are arithmetic, not clinical interpretation.
- The software does not automate any step of clinical care.
- The clinical rules executed by the software are authored either by the manufacturer (a curated subset of QOF and drug-monitoring rules, configurable by the practice) or by the practice itself — the software is a rules runner and display layer, not a clinical knowledge base.
- The Patient Record Visualiser's eFI score is an arithmetic count of matched problem-list terms against a published 36-deficit reference list — it is not a validated clinical assessment instrument as implemented in GP clinical systems.
- The Patient Record Visualiser's PINCER flags are a subset of the published PINCER prescribing safety criteria, surfaced as prompts to verify against the source record — they are supplementary to Medicus's own prescribing safety systems and do not replace them.
- The live Triage Lens prescribing-combination prompts (STOPP/START-style) are deterministic, name-based detections of co-prescribed drug classes, surfaced as prompts to review against the source record. They recommend no drug, dose, or change and are supplementary to Medicus's own prescribing-safety systems.
- The risk-tool chip provides hyperlinks to externally-hosted, independently-validated calculators (QRISK3, QCancer, eFI) together with a list of the inputs each requires; the software computes no risk score itself.
- The Pharmacy First signposting snippets are pre-written reference text and hyperlinks surfaced for the clinician's consideration. The clinician makes any signposting decision; the snippets state the pathway's eligibility gateway and red-flag safety-netting. They do not constitute a triage decision made by the software.
- The hyperkalaemia and falling eGFR trend alerts (v3.18.0) are arithmetic threshold and trend comparisons against NICE/KDIGO criteria — they surface a coded observation value against a published threshold; they do not diagnose acute kidney injury or hyperkalaemia, and they do not replace the clinician's direct review of the result in the Medicus record.
- The ACR Trend action banners, including the ACR ≥70 referral-trigger banner (v3.25.0), are threshold-based prompts to _consider_ a referral per NICE NG203; the software makes no referral and does not assess whether a referral has already been made or is appropriate for the individual patient.
- The vaccination eligibility chips (v3.26.0) infer eligibility and status from coded data as a prompt to check — they do not interact with the National Immunisation Management Service (NIMS) or any external vaccination record.
- The Condor operational dashboard (v3.35.0) displays administrative and operational practice metrics — demand/capacity ratios, submission velocity, a Practice Pressure Index, and task-inbox age — for situational awareness. These are arithmetic aggregations of administrative data, not clinical assessments of individual patients, and they make no clinical decisions.
- The Pre-clinic Monitoring Sweep (v3.37.0) and its reception handout (v3.44.0) run the existing Sentinel rules engine against the booked appointment list; they surface the same chip set as the live Sentinel module. The Sweep generates no clinical output beyond the same prompts already covered by existing modules. From v3.56.0 the Sweep additionally generates batch Action-Pack artefacts; these are copy-ready administrative booking/recall instructions produced by the same deterministic rules engine, not clinical recommendations.
- The Reception guided capture pathways (v3.38.0) present a fixed question set to non-clinical staff and produce a structured complaint description as a plain-text Medicus entry. The software makes no triage decision; all pathways ship disabled and require practice CSO/GP review before enablement.
- The Knowledge module (v3.42.0) stores and displays practice-owned reference entries. It is explicitly reference material, not clinical decision support.
- The Triage Lens red-flag engine expansion (v3.46.0) adds pattern-matching rules for additional clinical presentations; these are deterministic text-pattern detections that function as a supplementary aide-mémoire, not a clinical triage system.
- The SMR tab in the Patient Record Visualiser (v3.51.0) computes an ACB score (an arithmetic sum of per-drug anticholinergic burden scores from the Boustani scale) and applies a subset of STOPP/START v3 criteria as prompts to review against the source record. Neither the ACB score nor the STOPP/START flags are clinical decisions; both are supplementary to the clinician's own prescribing assessment and to Medicus's own prescribing-safety systems. The STOPP/START criteria implemented are a starter subset; absence of a flag does not guarantee prescribing safety. **(v3.114.0)** The anticholinergic-burden score is now computed by a single canonical scorer (`engine/acb-scores.js`) shared by both the live in-queue Triage Lens HUD and the record/Visualiser views, so the same patient receives the same ACB score on every surface (this closes a prior divergence in which the in-queue chip under-scored several strong anticholinergics — see `HAZARD-LOG.md` H-031); the STOPP NSAID and low-dose-aspirin name lists were brought to full UK class/brand parity; and both the ACB and STOPP class-term lists and the engine→chip rendering are now regression-guarded in CI (`test-term-coverage.js`, `test-chip-contract.js`). These remain supplementary review prompts, not clinical decisions, and absence of a flag still does not guarantee prescribing safety.
- The per-patient evaluation audit trail ("Why?" panel, v3.47.0) and the exportable evaluation log are diagnostic transparency tools; they record the rule engine's reasoning for display to the user and do not constitute a clinical assessment or a patient record.
- The Today tab (v3.60.0) is an arithmetic aggregation of the same administrative feeds already displayed by other modules (waiting room, triage counts, demand counts, slot availability); it performs no per-patient clinical evaluation beyond re-displaying the existing Sweep summary, and makes no clinical decisions.
- The guided tour, setup checklist, command palette, and view-state continuity (v3.57.0–v3.60.0) are user-interface chrome; they read no clinical data and produce no clinical output.
- Clinic mode (v3.60.0) is a user-controlled mute of the extension's own desktop pop-up notifications and sounds. It is enforced only at the desktop-notification emission points; it cannot suppress any on-screen strip, badge, chip, or in-page clinical surface. Muting one's own operational pop-ups is not a clinical function.

## 5. What the software DOES

In summary, Medicus Suite:

1. **Reads** data from the Medicus EPR via:
   - DOM scraping of the rendered Medicus web pages
   - Interception of page-level API responses using the browser's `PerformanceObserver` API (read-only observation)
   - Authenticated fetch calls to `*.api.england.medicus.health` endpoints, using the user's existing browser session cookies
2. **Reorganises and summarises** that data in a side panel and on configurable HUD overlays
3. **Applies arithmetic threshold checks** to monitoring intervals and QOF indicator criteria
4. **Displays** the result as colour-coded indicators (chips), aggregate counts, and tabular summaries
5. **Analyses a locally-held EPR export PDF** (Patient Record Visualiser only) to produce a multi-tab clinical dashboard including: continuity indices, investigation trends, drug monitoring compliance, eFI score, PINCER-style prescribing prompts, QOF register review status, and an SMR tab with ACB burden, STOPP/START v3 flags and a printable SMR skeleton — all computed locally in the browser, with no external transmission
6. **Checks the GitHub releases API** once a day for new versions of itself (no patient data is transmitted in this check)
7. **Self-monitors extraction health** by maintaining a rolling baseline of record-read counts per view and surfacing a global amber banner when a sustained drop below that baseline is detected — providing an additional signal that a Medicus layout change may have silently degraded data extraction
8. **Performs a small, enumerated set of user-initiated write actions** — appointment booking, general-task creation, inbound-document filing, normal-lab-result filing, routine-prescription re-assignment, and problem-list / duplicate-entry tidying. Each is listed, with its trigger and its controls, at **section 6.1**; none is automatic, scheduled, or performed without an explicit user action and confirmation

That is the entirety of the software's behaviour.

## 6. What the software DOES NOT do

Medicus Suite does **not**:

> **CORRECTION PENDING — raised at CSO review 2026-07-28, not yet incorporated into items 1, 8 and 9 below, and recorded here rather than left silent.** Those three items assert without qualification that nothing is transmitted externally. That is true of the **default** configuration and of every module described in this notice, but it is **not** true when a practice enables the optional **Medicus Transactional API** integration (`txn.integrationMode` = `hybrid` or `transactional`, off by default): patient reads are then routed through a Graysbrook-operated UK backend proxy which signs a short-lived Medicus token and forwards them, returning the GP Connect structured record and demographics by the same route. That path is read-only, dormant unless a practice deliberately configures a proxy URL and credential, and documented at `docs/TRANSACTIONAL-API-INTEGRATION.md`; it is described accurately in the frozen statement at `docs/INTENDED-PURPOSE.md` (v3.202.0, "Data flow and egress"). It carries no hazard-log entry and no DPIA assessment at the date of this note. **Until items 1/8/9 are rewritten, a hazard entry exists and the DPIA covers the transfer, a practice must not enable `hybrid` or `transactional` mode.** Everything else in this section stands as written.

1. **Transmit patient data to any external server.** All processing is local in the user's browser. No telemetry, no analytics, no remote logging, no cloud storage. *(Subject to the correction note above.)*
2. **Modify any record automatically, silently, or in the background.** The software does have a defined and limited set of write paths into Medicus — they are enumerated in full at section 6.1 — but every one of them is triggered by a user action on the record or task in front of them, is confirmed at the point of commit, and writes through Medicus's own endpoints or its own on-screen controls, so Medicus's validation, access control and audit trail apply exactly as if the user had done the work by hand. There is no scheduled, timed, or background write; no write the software initiates on its own; and no write outside the surfaces listed at 6.1. **Medicus remains the system of record** — the extension holds no record of its own and never becomes the authoritative copy of anything it writes.
3. **Write anything beyond those enumerated actions.** It does not modify QOF claims data, respond to referrals, prescribe, alter a prescription's clinical content, sign anything, or complete any Medicus workflow the user has not completed themselves. It composes no free-text message to a patient: the only patient-facing communication it can cause is Medicus's **own** booking- or cancellation-confirmation SMS/email. The booking surfaces (W1/W2/W12) currently pass every channel Medicus offers (W3). The appointment-organise cancel and move surfaces (W14/W15) send **empty** recipient lists by default; since v3.236.15 each staged action carries a per-action **"Tell the patient" opt-in** which, when ticked, copies the recipient Medicus's own form offers (never invented, fail-closed when no channel is offered) so Medicus sends its own confirmation — the confirm bar names exactly how many patients will be messaged. The stretch surface (W16) never sends recipients and offers no tick. Several surfaces are deliberately **prepare-only** — they compose text and stop: the GP→reception quick-actions composer (appends a sentence to the task's Internal comment box and highlights, never clicks, Medicus's Submit control — H-049), the "message patient" arm of normal-lab filing (pre-fills or copies a draft and never sends), the Reception guided-capture output and Action Packs (clipboard text the user pastes), and the Outstanding-Investigation "Select all" helper (ticks checkboxes only — limitation 38).
4. **Make prescribing recommendations.** It does not recommend any drug, dose, route, or duration.
5. **Make clinical decisions.** It does not diagnose, triage, prioritise, rank, or interpret patient data.
6. **Replace reading the source record.** Every value it displays must be verified against the Medicus record before any clinical action.
7. **Provide clinical decision support.** It is not CDS. It must not be represented as CDS.
8. **Store patient data persistently.** `chrome.storage.local` is used for configuration, discovered API URLs, aggregate counts, and the user's own rule definitions. The in-memory cache is volatile and keyed by patient UUID; it is discarded when the tab closes or the patient changes. The Visualiser's PDF analysis is entirely in-memory and is discarded when the tab closes. From v3.50.0–v3.60.0 a small set of **short-lived local working copies** containing patient data exist by design, each time-limited and excluded from suite backups: print payloads (reception handout, batch pack, patient passport — overwritten on each use, with a 60-second clear backstop for the passport), the reception capture draft (cleared on generation or discard; expires after 4 hours), and the last Sweep run (replaced on each run; expires after 2 hours). These are working copies on the local workstation only; nothing is transmitted externally and nothing enters the backup envelope.
9. **Store credentials.** It uses the user's existing Medicus session cookies; it does not request, store, or transmit a separate username or password.
10. **Operate outside Medicus.** It produces no useful output on any other EPR or non-Medicus web page.
11. **Act as a regulated medical device** of any class.
12. **Guarantee completeness of PDF parsing.** The Visualiser processes text-based PDF exports; entries rendered as images, tables, or using non-standard fonts may not be extracted.
13. **Reflect the live record in the Visualiser.** The Patient Record Visualiser operates on a point-in-time PDF export; it does not reflect changes made to the patient record after the export was generated.

### 6.1 The write paths, stated in full

Earlier issues of this notice stated that the software had "no write path to Medicus". **Principles that hold across every row of that table, and are conditions of use:**

1. **Medicus remains the system of record.** Nothing in this table makes the extension authoritative for anything. Where a write can be done by driving Medicus's own controls rather than by calling an endpoint (W7, W8), that is deliberately preferred, because it keeps Medicus's own validation, access control and audit trail in the loop.
2. **Medicus's validation, ACL and audit fire on every write.** All API writes are credentialed same-origin calls made under the signed-in user's own Medicus session — the user can write exactly what their Medicus permissions already allow them to write, attributed to them, and no more. The extension holds no credential of its own.
3. **Every write is user-initiated and explicitly confirmed.** No write is scheduled, timed, retried in the background, or performed on the user's behalf without their action. Write failures are surfaced, never silently retried.
4. **Identity discipline is the load-bearing control.** The dominant hazard on every one of these paths is writing to the wrong patient or the wrong task (H-043). The strongest form of the control — pin the identity, then **re-resolve and re-check it immediately before the commit** — originated at W2 and W5 and is the standard every write surface is being brought up to; where a surface does not yet have it (see the note at W1), that is stated in the table rather than papered over.
5. **The user is still accountable for the write.** Section 8 applies with full force: check the underlying Medicus record before booking, filing, ending, removing or re-assigning anything on the basis of what the extension shows.

## 7. Known limitations

The user and the deploying practice must understand and accept the following known limitations. A fuller account is in `docs/sentinel-DISCLAIMER.txt` and in `docs/HAZARD-LOG.md`.

### Side panel and Monitoring module

1. **QOF register membership is approximated.** Register matching is by substring of problem labels, not by SNOMED refset. Both false positives and false negatives occur.
2. **QOF coverage is a curated subset.** Not all QOF 2025/26 indicators are implemented. A blank panel does not mean all indicators are achieved — it may mean the indicator is not in the implemented set.
3. **Encounter-coded entries may be missed.** Certain coded entries (annual review codes, smoking status, mental health questionnaires) live on endpoints that the extension does not consume; indicators relying on them may report "no data".
4. **Annual QOF specification changes are not auto-detected.** The practice must review rule files at each QOF contract refresh.
5. **User-edited thresholds override the defaults.** If the practice edits a threshold, the practice is responsible for confirming it against current published guidance.
6. **Medicus API changes are not auto-detected.** If Medicus changes the shape of its API, the extension may show incomplete or no data until updated.
7. **The extension does not validate the correctness of the Medicus record.** If the source data is wrong, the displayed evaluation will be wrong.
8. **Practice-authored rules are the practice's responsibility.** Custom rules built via the form builder span five rule types (drug-monitoring, drug-combo, qof-indicator, event-count, composite). From v3.15.0–v3.16.0 the builder offers an engine-backed live preview (it evaluates the rule against an editable test patient using the real engine) and validates each rule against the engine schema on save — but the _clinical_ validity of the rule itself remains the responsibility of the practice author.
9. **Custom indicators are not QOF indicators** and do not contribute to any QOF income claim. They are visually labelled "Custom".

### Patient Record Visualiser

10. **The Visualiser operates on a PDF snapshot, not the live record.** Clinical information may have changed since the PDF was exported. The export date is displayed; users must ensure they are working from a current export.
11. **PDF parsing completeness is not guaranteed.** Entries rendered as images, in certain font types, or in non-standard Medicus export layouts may not be extracted. Entry counts are displayed so users can detect implausibly low figures.
12. **The eFI score is an approximation.** It is computed by matching problem-list text against a 36-deficit reference list using substring matching. It is not equivalent to the eFI as calculated by GP clinical systems from SNOMED-coded data. Both under- and over-estimation of frailty are possible. It is a screening aid only.
13. **PINCER flags are a partial implementation.** Only a defined subset of PINCER criteria are implemented (NSAID + CKD, NSAID + heart failure, NSAID + anticoagulant, beta-blocker + asthma, ACEi/ARB + CKD with overdue U&E, as at v3.16.0). Absence of a flag does not guarantee prescribing safety. Medicus's own prescribing safety systems remain the primary control.
14. **Drug detection is regex-based.** High-risk drug and PINCER drug detection works by text-matching PDF content. Brand names or abbreviated entries not in the implemented regex may be missed.
15. **High-risk drug monitoring intervals are defaults from NICE/BNF guidance.** They do not account for patient-specific monitoring plans, clinician-directed variation, or local protocol modifications.
16. **RCV delta flags are based on published reference change values.** They indicate statistically significant analytical change, not clinical significance in any individual patient's context.
17. **Clinical zone bands (eGFR, HbA1c, BP) are based on current published guidance.** They reflect KDIGO, NICE QOF, and NICE hypertension thresholds at the time of release; they do not account for patient-specific targets.

### Live Triage Lens prescribing prompts and signposting (v3.13.0–v3.14.0)

18. **STOPP/START prompts are a small, name-based subset.** The record-panel prescribing-combination prompts cover only a few well-established, low-false-positive combinations (NSAID + anticoagulant/antiplatelet; NSAID + ACEi/ARB + diuretic; benzodiazepine/Z-drug in age ≥80). They are not the full STOPP/START v2 criteria, detect drugs by name (brand, abbreviation, or coding variants may be missed), cannot account for the individual patient's indication, gastroprotection, monitoring, or specialist plan, and are supplementary to Medicus's own prescribing-safety systems. Absence of a prompt does not indicate prescribing safety.
19. **Pharmacy First signposting is conditional on eligibility the clinician must confirm.** The snippets surface "consider Pharmacy First if eligible" for the relevant pathways; each pathway is age/sex/clinically gated, the patient's age/sex may be unknown to the software, and the request text may be ambiguous. The clinician is responsible for confirming pathway eligibility and clinical suitability before signposting, and for acting on the red-flag safety-netting stated in the snippet.
20. **The risk-tool chip computes nothing.** It links to the official QRISK3 / QCancer / eFI calculators and lists the inputs each requires. The software calculates no risk score; the clinician enters the inputs into the external calculator and interprets the result.

### Applicability filters (all rule types)

21. **Demographic-gated rules fire when age or sex is unknown (fail-open, v3.12.1).** A rule restricted to a sex or age band will still fire when the patient's age/sex cannot be read from the page. This is deliberate, so that safety-critical alerts (for example the MHRA valproate alert) are not silently suppressed when the banner cannot be scraped; the consequence is that such a rule may occasionally appear for a patient who is in fact outside its intended scope. A rule is suppressed only when the patient is positively known to be out of scope. As always, verify against the source record.

### Per-rule hide/snooze (v3.26.3)

22. **Permanently hidden chips resurface only when their severity WORSENS — not on every change in the clinical picture.** *(Corrected 2026-08-22: this limitation previously said hidden chips never re-appear, which pre-dated the severity-escalation resurface now implemented in `chipSuppressionResult` — a chip permanently dismissed with a recorded status re-appears when its current severity ranks WORSE than at dismissal, and an unknown status ranks as red, fail-safe.)* Two residual gaps remain and are the point of this limitation: a chip whose status changes **without worsening in rank** (e.g. a different amber reason) stays hidden; and **legacy dismissals made before the status-at-dismissal field existed never resurface at all**. The "no chip ≠ all clear" principle applies with equal force to suppressed chips: their absence is not evidence that the monitoring has been done. Users must be briefed that hiding a chip does not mean the monitoring has been completed. The rules owner should review the suppression list periodically via the "Hidden / Snoozed Alerts" section in sentinel settings. Vaccine chip snoozes auto-expire at the season end and are not covered by this limitation.

### Vaccination eligibility alerts (v3.26.0)

23. **Vaccination eligibility and status are inferred from coded data.** Flu and COVID-19 eligibility chips are derived from QOF register membership, problem-list entries, medications, BMI, and age using JCVI/UKHSA 2025/26 criteria. DUE / GIVEN / DECLINED status is inferred from coded problems, observations, and journal entries within the current season window. Vaccinations administered outside the practice (pharmacy, community hub, hospital, other practice) may not be recorded locally, so a DUE chip may appear for a patient who is already vaccinated. Every vaccine chip carries a "DOUBLE-CHECK ELIGIBILITY" note and matched evidence; vaccination status must be verified against the Medicus record and, where relevant, national vaccination records (NIMS/PCSE) before any clinical action.

### Pre-clinic Monitoring Sweep module

26. **The Sweep module is a supplementary morning-huddle aid, not an audited monitoring record.** The Sweep module runs the same Sentinel rules engine against today's booked patients from the practice appointment book (`/scheduling/data/appointment-book/embedded-overview` — optionally filtered to a single clinician's diary; cancelled appointments excluded), using the same per-patient API reads (banner, medication regimen, problem listing, investigation dashboard) as the live Sentinel module. A sweep that finds no patients always states why (empty appointment book, empty clinician filter, or unreadable feed) — it never reports a bare zero. It is subject to all the limitations of those data sources (limitations 1–9 above). The following additional constraints apply:
    - **Point-in-time snapshot only.** Results reflect the record at the moment the sweep ran. Changes made to the record after the sweep (new bloods added, medications stopped, problems coded) are not reflected until the sweep is re-run.
    - **A patient that fails to load is never silently skipped.** If any patient's record cannot be fetched, a visible "could not read record" error is shown for that patient alongside the reason. An absent row is not a clean result.
    - **No alert ≠ monitoring complete.** Absence of an action-needed chip for a patient means no matched rule fired against the data returned at the time of the sweep; it is not confirmation that all monitoring is up to date. Limitations 1–9 (incomplete rule set, encounter-coded entries missed, API drift, etc.) apply equally here.
    - **Per-workstation hidden alerts are intentionally included.** The Sweep does not apply `sentinel.hiddenRules` suppressions. A chip permanently suppressed on a workstation (for example because the clinician has hidden it in the Sentinel panel) still contributes to the sweep worklist — hiding a rule in the live panel must not silently omit a patient from the morning recall list. When a patient's sweep result includes chips that are hidden in the Sentinel panel, this is flagged to the clinician.
    - **Results persist briefly for resume (v3.60.0) and then expire.** The last sweep run (including batch selections) is kept as a local working copy for up to 2 hours so that an accidental tab switch does not destroy the morning worklist; a "Resume last sweep" card shows the run time so the user can judge staleness. A resumed sweep is STILL the point-in-time snapshot from when it ran — records may have changed since; re-run rather than resume if in doubt. The stored run is replaced on every new run, expires after 2 hours, and is excluded from suite backups — see limitation 34 and H-027.
    - **Manual trigger only.** The sweep runs only when the clinician presses "Run sweep". There is no automatic or scheduled run.

### Reception module (guided capture, recent appointments, opportunity summary)

27. **The Reception module's guided capture is a structured history-taking aid for non-clinical staff — it is NOT a triage tool and makes no clinical decisions.** The capture pathways (`rules/reception-pathways.json`) present a fixed, identical-every-time question set per presenting problem, with red-flag questions asked first. The following constraints apply:
    - **Red-flag prompts are escalation triggers, not exclusions.** A "yes" to any red-flag question displays an immediate escalation instruction (999-level or duty-clinician-level) and stamps the generated text with the flag and the instruction. A full set of "no" answers does NOT mean the contact is safe to handle routinely — the red-flag lists are deliberately short, lay-phrased subsets of NICE CKS / NICE guideline red flags and cannot be exhaustive. The receiving clinician reviews every capture.
    - **The output is unverified caller-reported information** recorded by non-clinical staff. It must be treated as a structured complaint description, not as clinical findings.
    - **Wrong-patient paste risk.** The generated text is copied to the clipboard and manually pasted into Medicus. When a patient record is open, the patient's name/DOB is embedded in the text header specifically so a wrong-record paste is detectable on reading — staff must still verify the destination record before pasting.
    - **All pathways ship DISABLED.** Enabling any pathway requires a practice administrator to accept an explicit disclaimer in Options → Reception confirming that a clinician (CSO or nominated GP) has reviewed the pathway content and that staff have been briefed. Individual pathways can then be toggled on/off per practice.
    - **Pathway content requires CSO sign-off and periodic review.** The bundled pathway set is marked DRAFT until reviewed by the practice's clinical safety officer, and is included in The Keeper's periodic rule-currency review alongside the other rule files. Structural integrity is CI-guarded (`test-reception-pathways.js`), but clinical correctness of the question wording is a human review responsibility.
    - **Practice-edited and custom pathways are the deploying practice's responsibility** (the same stance as Sentinel custom rules). Edits to bundled pathways and practice-authored pathways are validated structurally (every red flag needs an escalation level; pathways cannot be saved without red flags) but their clinical wording is not checked by the software. An invalid edit is flagged and the bundled original stays active — it is never silently dropped. Re-review clinically after every edit.
    - **Backups can carry pathway content and enable flags.** Imported backups are validated and whitelist-sanitised by the same code path as the editor, and the import preview explicitly WARNS when a backup would enable capture pathways.
    - **The quick-wins status pill** on the Reception tab compresses the open patient's Sentinel chips to a single green/amber/red state. Practices can exclude individual rules from this view; when active alerts are filtered out, the expanded view says so ("N alert(s) not shown here by practice settings") — filtering is never silent. It is not a recall system, and green does not mean nothing is due (limitation 22 and the "no chip ≠ all clear" rule apply).
    - **Captured answers are draft-saved locally while a capture is in progress (v3.60.0).** To prevent loss of a 10–15 question phone capture on an accidental tab switch, the in-progress form is auto-saved as a local draft that is cleared on generation or explicit discard and expires after 4 hours; it is excluded from suite backups. Restoring a draft is an explicit user choice from a time-stamped banner — staff must confirm the draft belongs to the current contact before restoring (see limitation 34 and H-029). Generated text and taker initials are otherwise in-memory only and discarded when the panel closes. Stored configuration is limited to pathway enable flags, pathway content, and the chip filter (`reception.*` keys, included in suite backups).

### BP Trend and ACR Trend modules (v3.25.0)

24. **Trend data is available only after the Medicus investigation dashboard has loaded.** The BP Trend, ACR Trend, and all other views within the unified Trends tab source data from the Medicus investigation dashboard endpoint; if the investigation dashboard has not yet loaded in the current session, the tab will show no data. Trends require multiple historical readings — a patient with only one recorded value will show no trend. Trend data is cleared on every patient navigation (cross-patient guard) and therefore requires re-loading when switching between patients.

25. **ACR Trend action banners are threshold comparisons, not individualised clinical assessments.** The ACR ≥70 referral-trigger banner ("consider nephrology referral — NICE NG203"), the ACR doubling banner, and the KDIGO category-crossing banner are arithmetic comparisons against published NICE NG203 and KDIGO criteria. They do not account for individual patient context — existing specialist involvement, dialysis, recent biopsy, patient preference, or whether a referral has already been made. They are prompts to _consider and verify_, not referral recommendations. Verify against the full Medicus record and any active correspondence before acting.

### Condor operational dashboard (v3.35.0)

28. **Condor metrics are administrative and operational, not clinically authoritative.** The Condor dashboard's waiting-room count, demand/capacity ratio, submission velocity, Practice Pressure Index, and task-inbox age are derived from the same Medicus data feeds as other modules. They reflect the state of the appointment book and task list at the time of the most recent poll; they are not a substitute for direct review of operational workflows in Medicus. The Practice Pressure Index is an arithmetic composite of operational indicators; it does not assess clinical urgency, staffing adequacy, or patient safety. Staffing and workload decisions made on the basis of Condor metrics must be validated against the live Medicus record and the practice's own operational knowledge — see H-022 in the hazard log.

### Sweep batch Action-Pack generation (v3.56.0)

29. **Batch-generated Action-Pack artefacts are keyed to the patient at the time the Sweep was run.** From v3.56.0 the Sweep module can generate Action-Pack artefacts (blood-form requests, recall SMS messages, letters, tasks) for all action-needed patients identified in a single sweep run. Every artefact carries the patient's name and NHS number, and the sweep results display them per patient row so that a reception or admin user can verify the patient identity before acting on each one. However, batch generation introduces an additional wrong-patient risk compared with single-patient chip use: artefacts must not be acted upon without explicit per-patient identity verification against the Medicus record. The "no artefact ≠ monitoring complete" principle applies with equal force to the batch output as to the individual sweep — see limitation 26 and H-023 in the hazard log.

### Triage Lens free-text red-flag engine (v3.46.0 expansion)

30. **Absence of a Triage Lens red-flag chip is not absence of risk.** The expanded triage-lens rule set (52 additional rules from v3.46.0, DKA/HHS rule from v3.52.0, plus further additions) provides pattern-matching prompts for a broad range of clinical presentations. It is a supplementary aide-mémoire for non-clinical staff handling patient requests, not a clinical triage system. Missing a pattern in the request text, an ambiguous phrasing, a presentation outside the rule set, or a rule whose regex is not triggered by the specific wording used will all result in no chip — this is not a reassurance that the presentation is routine. Every contact must be reviewed by a clinician; the triage lens is a prompt to flag, not a decision tool. See H-019 and the prescribing-flag limitations at items 18–19.

### Extraction-health canary and per-module breakdown (v3.33.0 / v3.47.0)

31. **Extraction-health indicators are informational, not alarms.** The per-module extraction breakdown (`Extracted: N meds · N obs · N problems`, v3.33.0) and the rolling drift-detection canary (v3.47.0) are transparency aids that provide earlier warning of a partial or full Medicus scraper failure. A zero count in the breakdown is amber-tinted to prompt a manual check but is never treated as an alarm — a record can legitimately have no observations or no problems. The drift banner fires only on a sustained, multi-sample deviation from the historical baseline. Neither mechanism replaces direct verification of displayed values against the Medicus record before any clinical action.

### Clinic mode and the consolidated Notifications settings (v3.60.0)

32. **Clinic mode mutes the extension's desktop pop-ups and sounds only — and the user must remember it is on.** Clinic mode (Options → Notifications, the 🔕 panel pill, or Ctrl+K) temporarily silences the extension's own desktop notifications and notification sounds for a chosen period (30 minutes, 1 hour, or until 18:00 — a selection made after 18:00 rolls to the next day). It NEVER suppresses: the on-panel waiting-room/triage/demand strips, the toolbar badge, Sentinel chips, the Triage Lens HUD, or any in-module banner — these continue to update and display throughout. This boundary is enforced in code at the desktop-notification emission points only, and is stated in the settings UI. Residual considerations: a muted period delays _pop-up_ awareness of triage-threshold crossings (the strip still shows them — the user must glance at the panel); the active state is shown as a 🔕 pill in the panel header and expires automatically; alerts that fired during a muted period are recorded in the Recent alerts log on the Today tab. See H-028.

### Today tab and the recent-alerts log (v3.60.0)

33. **The Today tab is an administrative glance, not a clinical worklist.** Today aggregates the same administrative feeds shown elsewhere (waiting room, triage counts, demand counts, slot availability) plus the last Sweep summary, each subject to the data-currency constraints of its source module and to limitations 26 and 28. The Recent alerts card lists the extension's own operational alert events (channel, level, time — counts and labels only, never patient identifiers) and is a convenience record, not an audit trail: concurrent writes may occasionally drop an entry, the list is capped at 50, and it must not be relied upon as evidence that an alert did or did not fire.

### Local working copies: reception drafts and sweep resume (v3.60.0)

34. **Restored drafts and resumed sweeps are point-in-time working copies — verify context before reuse.** A restored reception draft contains answers given by a previous caller contact; staff must confirm the draft belongs to the current contact (the restore banner shows the saved time, and restoring is an explicit choice) and discard it otherwise. A resumed sweep reflects the records as they were when the sweep ran (≤2 hours earlier); bloods added, medications changed, or appointments altered since are not reflected — re-run rather than resume when currency matters. Both working copies expire automatically (4h / 2h), are cleared on completion, never leave the workstation, and are excluded from suite backups. See H-027 and H-029.

### Investigation Results queue triage chips (v3.62.0)

35. **Investigation Results queue triage chips are a prioritisation aid, not assurance (v3.62.0).** On the Medicus Investigation Results filing queue the Triage Lens shows per-row chips — urgent and abnormal (both derived solely from the laboratory’s own reference-range and urgent-review flags on each report), an under-prioritised flag (lab/requester priority lower than the result severity), and an unmatched-patient flag. There is deliberately no “normal” or “safe to file” chip: **the absence of a chip means nothing has been asserted, not that the result is normal.** The chips never file a result, change its status, or take any action. Chip enable/disable and colour (severity kind) are user-configurable via the systemChips editor. From v3.63.0 users may additionally author analyte-threshold rules that escalate result severity (for example “Potassium ≥6.0 → red”); such rules are validated on save/import, arrive disabled when imported (including LLM-generated rules), and must be reviewed and enabled by a clinician before they fire. They can only raise a chip to amber or red — they can never lower or suppress the laboratory’s own urgent or abnormal flags; the engine takes the more-severe of lab-flag vs user-rule. This is the first capability in the extension that applies a clinical threshold it computes itself, rather than purely reflecting lab flags; the review-gate and escalate-only design are the material safety controls. From v3.64.0 result severity may additionally be raised by **text-classification rules** for microbiology/culture results, which carry no numeric high/low flag. A built-in MSU/urine-culture text rule ships enabled with a conservative normal-phrase list: it flags any matched culture “Needs review” (amber) unless the combined result text (result value, interpretation, lab comments) contains a configured normal phrase such as “No growth”, in which case it shows a calm “No growth” **info chip** instead. This info chip is an assertion of a negative culture from the actual result text — it is **not** a verdict that the result is safe to file. Clinicians must remain alert to known edge cases: **sterile pyuria** (no growth on culture but significant pyuria is present) and **”no significant growth — repeat if symptomatic”** (contains a no-growth phrase but the laboratory itself recommends a repeat). The normal-phrase list is deliberately conservative and is clinician-editable. User-authored text rules (manual or LLM-imported) arrive disabled and must be reviewed and enabled by a clinician before they fire, under the same review-gate model as threshold rules. Text rules are escalate-only: they can only raise a row to amber or show an info chip; they can never lower or suppress the laboratory’s own urgent or abnormal flags. Matching is case-insensitive substring against the result name (to select cultures) and the combined result text (to find the normal phrase). The absence of a chip still asserts nothing. Every result must still be opened and reviewed in Medicus; the chips only help order that work. If a report’s detail cannot be fetched, no chip is shown for that row.

### Record tab (v3.109.0)

36. **The Record tab is a live snapshot, not a complete record.** The Record tab shows a live API snapshot of the open patient — demographics, problems, medications (with overdue/review flags), recent results, ACB/STOPP prompts, and the live drug-monitoring and QOF chips. It carries a persistent "live snapshot, not a complete record" banner. Sections the suite does not retrieve — **allergies, immunisations and consultations** — render as explicit **gap-markers**: their absence is shown as "not retrieved", **never** as "none". An omitted section must therefore never be read as a negative finding (no known allergies, not immunised, no recent consultation). The "Copy summary" action (v3.114.0) emits plain text with a provenance header and a no-fabrication caveat; a copied summary is still a partial, point-in-time view and must not be treated as a complete record. Verify every value against the live Medicus record before any clinical action. See H-032.

### CQC Inspection Readiness (v3.110.0)

37. **The CQC Inspection Readiness pack is supporting evidence, not proof of compliance or population coverage.** The page turns the shipped rule-set and currency dates into CQC evidence (an internal readiness check and a gated, inspector-facing export). It is built from shipped rule data only — the suite is read-only and **cannot enumerate patient cohorts** — so the pack reflects what rules ship and when reference data was current, **not** that the practice's actual patient population has been audited. It must not be presented to an inspector or manager as proof of compliance or of population coverage. The export is gated behind an explicit confirmation, carries a "supporting evidence, not proof" disclaimer, and (from v3.114.0) includes "your count: \_\_\_" reconciliation columns that the practice must complete itself. See H-033.

### Outstanding Investigation tick-off (smart-match pipeline; formerly "Select all", v3.108.0)

38. **Ticking an outstanding-investigation checkbox IS the Medicus action — it commits server-side immediately and cannot be reversed from the extension.** *(Corrected 2026-08-22: this limitation previously described the removed v3.108.0 "Select all" helper and claimed ticking "never files, clears, or submits anything" — that was true of the old helper's page but is NOT true of the current Review Investigation Report page, where Medicus commits each tick as it is made.)* The current pipeline annotates request rows the engine matches to the report. The **clinician-confirmed bulk tick-off** presents a named list in a confirmation that **defaults to Cancel** and states the irreversibility in its own text; only that confirm ticks. The **auto-tick** arm ticks confident, report-covered rows with no per-instance confirmation and is therefore **off by default** — a practice that opts in accepts machine-initiated ticking under the H-036 controls (confidence + predating gates, once-per-task guard, audit log, persistent review toast). A wrongly-cleared chase-up must be re-requested in Medicus directly. See W22 in §6.1, H-034 and H-036.

### Signing Queue (v3.155.0–v3.159.0)

39. **Signing Queue monitoring context is not a "safe to sign" verdict.** The Signing Queue shows, next to each open prescription-request task, the same drug-monitoring chips Monitoring and Sweep already display for that patient, sorted riskiest-first. There is deliberately **no affirmative "safe"/"clear" state**: the absence of a flag means no monitoring rule matched the data the software could read — it does not mean monitoring is up to date. A record that fails to read shows as an amber "record not read — check manually" row and always ranks above quiet rows, so it can never be mistaken for a clear one. The module has no write path of any kind; every task resolves to its own patient UUID before any verdict is attached, so a verdict can never be shown against the wrong patient's tasks. Signing itself happens exclusively on Medicus's own signing screen, with Medicus's own safeguards. From v3.156.0 a verbatim, always-dated eGFR fact and, from v3.157.0, a verbatim collection-location chip appear per row — both are recorded facts only, with no threshold, band, or clinical logic computed from them. See H-038.

### Quick Leaflet popover and Leaflets tab (v3.154.0)

40. **Leaflet search results must be read before acting on them, not assumed from the query.** The Leaflets tab and the Quick Leaflet popover (reachable from any panel tab and the command palette) perform fuzzy text search over a bundled NHS A-Z leaflet index; the matched entry's full title is always shown before any action, and selecting it opens or copies a link to the corresponding page on nhs.uk. The search uses no patient data and makes no suggestion based on the patient being viewed — the clinician's typed query is the sole input. A fuzzy match can surface a similarly-worded but different entry; the full title must be checked against the intended topic before opening, printing, or sharing it with a patient. See H-039.

### Suite self-diagnosis (health strip) acknowledge/snooze (v3.154.1–v3.154.2)

41. **Dismissing the suite-health warning snoozes only that exact problem, for 7 days.** The amber "Medicus may have changed" strip can be dismissed for 7 days; the snooze is tied to the precise set of currently-degraded checks, so if anything changes — a new problem appears, or the mix of problems changes — the warning reappears immediately regardless of an earlier dismissal. Options → Suite health always shows the full current detail regardless of any snooze in effect.

### Patient Alerts — practice-recorded per-patient flags (v3.175.0)

42. **Patient Alerts are the practice's own notes, not the clinical record, and their absence is never an all-clear.** The Pt Alerts tab lets practice users attach their own flags to a patient (interpreter required, safeguarding concern, behaviour flags, or free text); the flags reappear on the alert strip, the Monitoring banner, and (from v3.176.0) as a banner inside Medicus's own patient header and as chips on task-queue rows, whenever that patient's record or task is on screen. They are stored only in the browser profile (shareable via backup files, which contain patient-identifiable data and must be handled accordingly) — anything clinically significant must still be coded in Medicus, where colleagues without the extension can see it. Flags are looked up against the live patient identity on every render and are never matched on name alone; when the patient cannot be identified, nothing is shown — so an absent flag means "none recorded or patient not identified", never a verified "no concerns". Flags have no expiry: the practice must review and remove flags (especially behavioural ones) that no longer have a basis. From v3.176.0 each flag records who added and last edited it, and every change is logged in the machine-local Event Ledger, so 'who put this here, when' is always answerable. See H-042.

### Triage North Star wave 1 — queue context chips and the breach-risk strip (v3.177.0)

43. **The urgent breach-risk strip echoes Medicus's own urgency flag and its absence is not an all-clear.** The strip counts open requests that Medicus itself has flagged urgent and warns when the oldest has waited ≥2h (amber) / ≥4h (red). It performs no clinical reading of request content: a clinically urgent request the intake process mislabelled routine is invisible to it, and a hidden strip means only "no Medicus-flagged-urgent request is over threshold", never "no urgent work exists". If the request data carries no readable priority field, the strip shows "urgency unknown — check queue" rather than a silent zero. When the Request Monitor is disabled or unconfigured, the strip is entirely silent (inert) — an absent strip must never be read as "urgent breaches are being watched". It can never suppress, reorder, or hide any request. See H-045.
44. **Pending-result cross-link chips are same-session pointers, not a results inbox.** A request-queue row chips "pending urgent/abnormal result" only when *this browser tab session* graded that patient's report on the results queue; the chip always states the age of that grading ("graded 12m ago") and vanishes when its source evaluation expires or is invalidated. Absence of the chip never means the patient has no pending abnormal results — results not graded this session are simply unknown to it. The underlying result must always be opened and reviewed in Medicus. See H-046.
45. **Repeat-contact chips count only what this workstation observed.** "3rd contact in 14d" comes from a rolling, machine-local ledger of patient/task identifiers and dates (no names, no request content, never included in backups); "N open requests · this patient" counts only tasks actually seen and resolved this tab session. Counts are per-browser-profile observations, not a practice-wide contact history: absence of the chip never means the patient has not been in contact repeatedly. See H-047.
46. **Carry-over chips date a task from when this workstation first saw it.** "Carried over Nd" fires only when the extension's own first-sighting predates the date shown on the row (the signature of a task whose displayed date reset on re-assignment); it defers to the standard task-age chip whenever the row's own date already shows the stronger signal. It is a per-workstation observation and cannot see tasks handled entirely on other machines. See H-048.

## 8. The single most important safety rule

> **Do not take a clinical action — ordering a test, making a referral, adjusting a medication, coding a QOF indicator, modifying a treatment plan — on the basis of what the extension shows without first checking the underlying Medicus record.**

This is a binding condition of use, and it is the primary patient safety control for this software. It is not optional. It applies to every user, in every session, on every patient, and to all outputs of the Patient Record Visualiser as well as the live side panel.

The source of clinical truth is the live Medicus record. The extension is a memory aid and analytical tool that sits next to it.

## 9. Responsibilities of the deploying GP practice

In the spirit of DCB0160, the deploying organisation (the practice) accepts the following clinical safety responsibilities. These should be discharged by the practice's nominated Clinical Safety Officer or, if none, by the senior partner.

### 9.1 Before deployment

1. **Read and accept** this notice, the hazard log, and the full disclaimer.
2. **Confirm regulatory and contractual fit.** Confirm that use of a third-party browser extension that reads from Medicus is consistent with the practice's contract with Medicus Health Ltd and with the practice's information governance policy. The author asserts but does not warrant such fit.
3. **Brief users.** Every user must be briefed on:
   - the intended purpose of the extension
   - the "single most important safety rule" in section 8 above
   - the known limitations in section 7
   - the additional limitations of the Patient Record Visualiser (sections 7.10–7.17)
   - the Sweep and batch artefact limitations (section 7, limitation 26 and 29)
   - the Reception module limitations and the requirement for CSO/GP pathway review before enablement (section 7, limitation 27)
   - the Condor dashboard limitations (section 7, limitation 28)
   - the "absence of a chip is not absence of risk" principle as it applies to all chip surfaces including the triage-lens (section 7, limitations 2, 26, 29, 30)
   - the clinic-mode boundary — desktop pop-ups and sounds only; strips, badges and clinical alerts are never muted — and the need to glance at the panel during a muted period (section 7, limitation 32)
   - the point-in-time nature of restored reception drafts and resumed sweeps (section 7, limitation 34)
   - the practice's incident-reporting route (see 9.3)
4. **Nominate a rules owner.** A named clinician should own the practice's Monitoring rule set, including any custom rules, and review it at every QOF contract refresh and after any significant change to clinical guidance affecting bundled rules.
5. **Review browser environment.** Confirm Google Chrome stable channel is the practice's standard browser, that the Chrome side panel API is not blocked by group policy, and that workstations are managed under the practice's normal IT security policy.

### 9.2 During use

1. **Curate the rule set.** Avoid enabling rules that are not clinically reviewed for the practice's case mix; over-broad rule sets contribute to alert fatigue (H-012).
2. **Verify source records.** Reinforce in induction and ongoing training that every value must be verified against Medicus before any clinical action.
3. **Monitor for anomalies.** Encourage users to report unexpected chips, missing chips, or wrong-patient display promptly.
4. **Keep the extension up to date.** When a new version banner appears, the rules owner should review the changelog before updating, then update at a quiet time.
5. **Ensure fresh PDFs for the Visualiser.** When using the Patient Record Visualiser, confirm that the PDF being analysed is a current export. Reuse of old PDFs is a documented clinical hazard (H-013).

### 9.3 If an incident occurs

1. **Stop using the affected module** and verify the source record.
2. **Manage any patient-safety implications** through the practice's significant event analysis (SEA) process.
3. **Report the technical anomaly to the manufacturer** at **dave@graysbrook.co.uk** with the date, time, extension version, module, observed output and expected output. **Do not transmit patient-identifiable data by email.**

| **High**     | Reproducible false-positive or false-negative clinical indicator that may have led to a clinical action; missed PINCER flag that was clinically significant | Within 3 working days  |
| **Medium**   | Reproducible display, layout or rule-engine bug not directly affecting clinical safety                                                                      | Within 10 working days |
| **Low**      | Cosmetic, feature request, documentation correction                                                                                                         | Best effort            |

Where a report meets the definition of a patient safety incident in NHS England guidance, the practice must also manage it under its own SEA process and, where applicable, report it via Learn from Patient Safety Events (LFPSE) and to the practice's ICB.

The CSO will:

- Acknowledge each report
- Investigate and reproduce where possible
- Issue a hot-fix release where appropriate
- Update the hazard log if a new hazard is identified or a control proves inadequate
- Notify all known users of any safety-relevant change
